# The Checkride Oral: Connecting Knowledge, Risk, and Skill — research packet

## Scope

- Audience: U.S. private-pilot airplane learners, especially ASEL applicants preparing to connect their study subjects in an oral conversation.
- Planned duration: 45–60 minutes; the user expressly allowed exceeding the normal target in favor of conversational teaching.
- Core question: How does a pilot use current evidence to explain a flight decision, then update that decision when a relevant input changes?
- Production-plan anchor: Core-25, The Checkride Oral: Connecting Knowledge, Risk, and Skill; scenario-based integration rather than last-minute memorization; whole ACS.
- Coverage boundary: Representative integration across applicable ACS tasks. This is a practice dialogue, neither an official/predictive examiner script nor an exhaustive practical-test rehearsal. Full preparation still covers every applicable task with the applicant's instructor.
- Aircraft scope: Fictional familiar ASEL trainer; no model-specific speeds, capacities, equipment architectures, or checklist actions asserted. The actual AFM/POH supplies aircraft-specific limitations and procedures.
- Narrative role: Instructor asks calm examiner-style questions; Learner rehearses Elena's answers and occasionally steps out of role to ask how to prepare. Elena is already a private pilot in the fictional passenger trip, explicitly distinguished from an applicant's test eligibility.

## Voice and teaching review of recent scripts

Read the complete master scripts for Episodes 21–24. Their common voice is calm, practical, and specific to a decision the pilot can make. Elena and Jonah provide continuity; preparation precedes the in-flight demand; the Learner asks plausible next questions; Instructor explanations make the needed connection; Announcer turns use the section heading and orient briefly. Each episode closes by rebuilding the same chain in Retrieval review, preserving the conditions that make each conclusion true.

Core-25 retains those features and increases alternating question/answer turns. The Instructor plays a cooperative practice examiner rather than an adversarial questioner. Selected operational Learner answers lead with evidence and pilot actions instead of describing the ACS repeatedly. Explicit ACS framing remains at the opening, occasional connections, and actual-test-process questions. The Learner can competently answer already-studied subjects, while the approach section explicitly asks for the missing physical explanation before making the angle-of-attack conclusion.

## Primary scenario trace before independent review

1. State the proposed daytime trip, relaxed schedule, and role distinction.
2. Establish pilot records, proficiency, fitness, and pressure handling.
3. Resolve the failed installed light before proceeding.
4. Study current weather, routes, airspace, Pine Valley, and the reservoir decision point.
5. Correct loading, calculate performance, and revisit runway selection after the wind changes.
6. Calculate the complete fuel plan, the alternative route, and additional personal margin before engine start.
7. Organize the cockpit and brief Jonah; prepare navigation resources before taxi.
8. Taxi using the actual route and clarify an uncertain instruction before proceeding.
9. Compare airborne progress with the plan; update time/fuel/weather and use prepared resources after tablet loss.
10. Reach the prepared weather decision point and divert promptly to the verified suitable Pine Valley option.
11. Pause explicitly for two separate failure variations, then return to the functioning-airplane main scenario.
12. Reassess arrival, explain the wing's demand before selecting the go-around, fly a stable second approach, land, and complete postflight.

No late-flight vignette requires unexplained preflight outputs. The systems and engine variations are announced as separate cases and explicitly closed before returning to the main arrival.

## Source-contract self-audit

Checked every source-tagged paragraph against the actor, action, scope, condition, and exact target page or subsection. ACS task requirements remain distinguished from actual regulatory conditions, handbook guidance, and fictional decisions. Adjacent factual paragraphs relying on different pages or authorities are split and separately tagged. Scenario airport suitability, repairs, weather progression, personal margins, and arithmetic remain expressly fictional or teaching synthesis.

The audit identified and removed a broadened normal-takeoff claim: PA.IV.A.R1 names aircraft performance/limitations, distance, and wind; pilot capability appears in the normal-landing task instead. The final normal-takeoff answer preserves the source's scope. The Retrieval review retains airplane VFR, wind/forecast consideration, first landing point, normal cruising speed, and day/night conditions of 91.151. It retains the coordinated-level-turn condition for the load/stall relationship and the single-generating-source/typical-light-airplane condition for the electrical example.

Arithmetic checks: 1.5 hours × 8 gallons/hour = 12 gallons; 24 − 12 = 12 gallons; 12 ÷ 8 = 1.5 hours = 90 minutes. Ninety nautical miles ÷ 75 knots = 1.2 hours = 72 minutes; compared with 90 knots, the extra 12 minutes at 8 gallons/hour uses 1.6 gallons. The second example explicitly resets position and actual fuel rather than reusing the first example's point.
