# VFR Operating Rules and Preflight Responsibilities — research packet

## Scope

- Audience: U.S. private-pilot airplane learners working through a typical daytime, non-turbine, standard-category rental airplane trip with one adult passenger.
- Target duration: about 37 minutes based on approximately 5,279 spoken words; this is an editorial estimate, not rendered audio.
- Core question: What does Elena have to establish about this airplane, this route, and Jonah before beginning the flight, and how do the operating rules affect her choices en route?
- Out of scope: the aircraft document, inspection, and inoperative-equipment method taught in Episode 8, *Aircraft Documents, Airworthiness, Inspections, and Maintenance*; special flight permits; special airworthiness certificates; airspace entry and VFR weather minimum tables; right-of-way tables; full cross-country calculations; and detailed emergency-procedure training.

## Likely trouble spots

- A rental dispatch is treated as Elena's safe-flight determination. Section 91.7 places that determination on the PIC; the operator's information helps her make it. The script refers the detailed document and equipment method to Episode 8.
- A current runway length is treated as useful even though the runway will be closed at expected arrival. Current availability and performance information must be applied to the actual intended airport and airplane. Source: 91.103(b).
- Two people and their bags are assumed to fit the replacement airplane, or full tanks are assumed to be harmless after the fuel stop. Current airplane loading data must establish total weight and CG for the actual fuel and occupants; reducing fuel to address weight changes range and the fuel plan. Source: PHAK Ch. 10, pp. 10-2 and 10-4; ACS PA.I.F.S1.
- A planned fuel reserve is treated as available trip fuel. Section 91.151(a) sets the minimum when beginning a VFR airplane flight; Elena makes an earlier operational fuel-stop decision when actual groundspeed erodes her extra margin. Source: 91.151(a).
- A 45-minute night fuel reserve is treated as permission for Elena to carry Jonah into night. She lacks the §61.57(b) experience for the later passenger window, has chosen a stricter home-before-sunset cutoff, and has not verified the replacement airplane against §91.205(a) and (c) for night VFR. Extra fuel addresses none of those gaps.
- The restraint instructions are treated as the entire passenger briefing. Section 91.107 specifies restraint briefing, notice, and use; ACS PA.II.B.S2 calls for a wider appropriate briefing that also covers PIC, doors, conduct, sterile aircraft, propeller avoidance, and emergency procedures.
- Five hundred feet above farmland is carried unchanged over a town. The altitude rule changes with the character of the area below, and the anywhere safe-landing condition remains relevant. Source: 91.119(a)–(c).
- The VFR semicircular altitude is chosen from heading or from height above mean sea level alone. Section 91.159 uses magnetic course and applies in level VFR cruise more than 3,000 feet above the surface, with its stated exceptions. Source: 91.159.

## Source classification and bounds

- The ACS states the applicant outcome; it does not itself impose the Part 91 operating rules.
- eCFR section links supply the regulatory requirements. Fictional Elena/Jonah choices are teaching synthesis and must not be described as FAA instructions or as evidence that a particular real airplane is airworthy.
- The exact installed configuration, maintenance status, and performance data are airplane-specific. The draft deliberately does not declare the blank moving map dispatchable or invent a runway-distance calculation.
- The replacement airplane's empty weight, loading limits, CG envelope, fuel capacity, and passenger and baggage weights are fictional but unspecified. The script teaches the check without inventing a loading calculation or claiming that full tanks fit.
- Elena's home-before-sunset cutoff is her stated trip choice from Episode 21. Section 61.57(b)'s passenger recency window starts one hour after sunset; the script must not present her earlier cutoff as a federal requirement. The replacement airplane's night equipment has not been established in this fictional scenario.
- AC 91-92 may be a useful supplemental study link for building a preflight briefing, but the script's required-action claim uses 91.103 rather than the advisory circular.

## Open technical questions

- None carried into the first complete draft. The unresolved display in the story is intentionally a pilot decision to stop and switch airplanes, not an unresolved research question.
