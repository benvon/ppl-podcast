# Core 03 research packet: Stalls, Load Factor, and Spin Avoidance

## Scope

Teach the relationship among angle of attack, airspeed, load factor, power, weight/CG, configuration, attitude, and yaw. The episode is awareness and decision education; it does not instruct a listener to practice stalls or spins, and it deliberately defers aircraft-specific procedures to the approved POH/AFM and a qualified instructor.

## ACS mapping

- **PA.VII.A.K1/R1/R3:** slow-flight aerodynamics, inadvertent slow flight, and uncoordinated flight.
- **PA.VII.B.K1-K4/R1/R5:** power-off-stall relationships, cues, prevention, recovery fundamentals, and secondary/accelerated/cross-control stall risk.
- **PA.VII.C.K1-K4/R1/R5:** the equivalent power-on-stall knowledge and risk framework.
- **PA.VII.D.K1-K3/R1:** spin aerodynamics, phases/causes, recovery procedure awareness, and inadvertent-spin risk.

## Teaching decisions

1. Start with the usable model: a stall is critical angle of attack exceeded, not a number on the airspeed indicator.
2. Explain load factor without treating bank angle as a magic trigger. Bank, pitch, G load, airspeed, and configuration are related but not interchangeable.
3. Make the base-to-final overshoot scenario a decision lesson: do not try to rescue an unstable turn with cross-control; go around when appropriate.
4. State the recovery priority as an aerodynamic concept, then stop short of publishing a generic operational checklist. The approved procedure for the aircraft controls.
5. Treat spin recovery as ACS knowledge and manufacturer-specific procedure, not something this episode teaches a listener to perform.

## Common misconceptions to correct

| Misconception | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| "Above the published stall speed means I cannot stall." | Published stall speed represents defined conditions. A stall occurs at critical angle of attack; the airspeed where that happens changes with conditions. |
| "The airplane has to look nose-high to stall." | An airplane can stall in many attitudes. The relevant aerodynamic condition is angle of attack. |
| "Add power first." | Power can help reduce altitude loss, but it does not by itself unstall the wing. The immediate aerodynamic priority is reducing angle of attack. |
| "I should pick up a dropped wing with aileron immediately." | First reduce angle of attack and maintain coordination; then restore the desired flightpath using the approved procedure. |
| "A spin is just a steep spiral." | A spin is a stalled, autorotating condition. A spiral dive is a different, unstalled condition that can rapidly build airspeed and load. |
| "Any training airplane can be spun." | Intentional spin approval and procedures are aircraft-specific. Consult the approved documents and limitations. |
