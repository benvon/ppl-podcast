# Weather Theory: Pressure, Stability, Moisture, and Clouds

**Episode:** core-11
**Version:** 0.1.1
**Source verification:** Deterministic FAA link, deep-citation, and mapping validation passed on August 28, 2026; formal LLM relevance review remains pending before render.

## In this episode

- Distinguish pressure, temperature, and density, then connect unequal heating with atmospheric motion.
- Follow a rising parcel through lower pressure, expansion, cooling, stability, saturation, and cloud formation.
- Separate water-vapor content, relative humidity, dew point, and visible moisture.
- Read layered and vertically developing clouds as evidence about stability, lift, ceiling, visibility, and precipitation.
- Carry the physical model into later VFR weather reports, forecasts, and go/no-go or continue/divert decisions.

## Study materials and visual aids

- [PHAK pressure reference and altitude relationship](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/14_phak_ch12.pdf#page=5) — Figure 12-6 and the surrounding text show the standard sea-level pressure reference and explain why pressure decreases with altitude.
- [PHAK temperature, dew point, and approximate cloud-base example](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/14_phak_ch12.pdf#page=14) — Figure 12-20 and the worked example show saturation and the simplified temperature-dew point convergence model.
- [PHAK basic cloud families and vertical development](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/14_phak_ch12.pdf#page=16) — Figure 12-22 organizes low, middle, high, and vertically developing clouds.
