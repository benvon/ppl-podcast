# Aeronautical Decision-Making and Risk Management — research packet

## Scope

- **Audience:** U.S. private-pilot airplane learners, especially pre-solo through practical-test preparation.
- **Target duration:** 30-35 minutes; approximately 4,800-5,000 spoken words at the selected unhurried production pace.
- **Core question:** How does a pilot turn changing facts, personal limits, and outside pressure into a safer plan or a safer change of plan?
- **In scope:** ADM; hazard versus risk; SRM; PAVE; 3P with CARE and TEAM; personal minimums; hazardous attitudes; scenario-based application.
- **Out of scope:** Aircraft-specific operating procedures; numeric personal-minimums prescriptions; weather-product interpretation; medical certification; legal advice; a maneuver lesson.

## Regulatory and source posture

- **Regulation:** 14 CFR 61.105(b)(12) identifies ADM and judgment as private-pilot aeronautical knowledge. Section 61.105(b)(13)(ii) includes planning alternatives if a flight cannot be completed or is delayed. This episode does not convert FAA guidance models into regulations.
- **FAA standard:** ACS Area I Task H covers ADM, SRM/CRM as appropriate, hazardous attitudes, distraction/bias risks, and self-assessment. Area II Task A covers pilot, aircraft, environment, and external pressures. Appendix 2 explains scenario-based ADM assessment throughout the practical test.
- **FAA guidance:** PHAK Chapter 2 provides the explanatory spine. Its current 2025 PHAK addendum concerns Chapter 1 material; this Episode 1 packet uses Chapter 2.

## Likely trouble spots

- **Misconception:** “ADM is just a go/no-go decision before departure.”
  - **Correction:** FAA guidance treats risk management and 3P as a continuous loop; the ACS includes continuing assessment of the environment.
  - **Source:** `phak-3p-care-team`, `phak-personal-minimums-followthrough`, `acs-preflight-assessment`.
- **Misconception:** “If a flight is legal, it is automatically wise for me today.”
  - **Correction:** Legal limits, aircraft limitations, and individual proficiency are different categories. Personal minimums are individual guardrails, never permission to exceed a regulation or approved aircraft limitation.
  - **Source:** `phak-pave-personal-minimums`, `acs-human-factors`.
- **Misconception:** “Personal minimums are fixed forever, or I can relax them today because I want this trip to happen.”
  - **Correction:** Treat personal minimums as a training objective: revisit them deliberately as experience and demonstrated proficiency change, outside the pressure of a particular trip. Do not negotiate them upward to continue the flight at hand.
  - **Source:** `phak-pave-personal-minimums`, `phak-personal-minimums-followthrough`.
- **Misconception:** “A risk score or acronym makes the decision.”
  - **Correction:** Models expose facts and alternatives; the pilot still assesses, chooses, acts, and evaluates.
  - **Source:** `phak-3p-care-team`, `phak-personal-minimums-followthrough`.
- **Misconception:** “External pressure means only a pushy passenger.”
  - **Correction:** Schedule, sunk cost, self-image, and an expectation about the weather can all distort the assessment.
  - **Source:** `acs-human-factors`, `phak-hazardous-attitudes`, `phak-pave-personal-minimums`.
- **Misconception:** “As a single pilot, I have no crew resources.”
  - **Correction:** SRM includes onboard and outside resources, while the pilot in command still owns the decision.
  - **Source:** `phak-risk-srm`.

## Scenario boundary

The script uses a fictional VFR cross-country scenario: a recently current student/private-pilot learner plans to fly to an unfamiliar airport where conditions are legal VFR but trending less comfortable, with a time-sensitive family event. It deliberately avoids real weather interpretation, concrete minimums, performance calculations, and specific operating procedures. The scenario demonstrates decision quality, not a universal go/no-go answer.
