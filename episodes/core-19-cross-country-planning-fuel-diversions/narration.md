Before beginning a VFR flight in an airplane, there must be enough fuel, considering wind and forecast weather, to reach the first point of intended landing and then, assuming normal cruising speed, fly for at least thirty minutes by day or forty-five minutes at night.

For this fictional daytime flight, thirty minutes at eight point five gallons per hour is four point two five gallons. Adding that to thirteen point nine gallons of planned trip fuel means at least eighteen point one five gallons must be aboard before departure under the stated assumptions. The pilot’s separate one-hour landing-fuel floor is an in-flight decision margin for this scenario, rather than part of section ninety-one point one five one.

**INSTRUCTOR:**

Who is responsible for the VFR flight plan?

**LEARNER:**

A filed VFR plan contains the required aircraft, pilot, route, destination, elapsed-time, endurance, and persons-aboard information. The regulation also requires notification to Flight Service or ATC when an activated plan is canceled or completed.

The AIM says the pilot is responsible for activating the VFR flight plan.

The pilot also ensures the VFR plan is closed after landing. A tower does not close it automatically.
