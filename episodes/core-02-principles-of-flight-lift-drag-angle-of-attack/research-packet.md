# Principles of Flight: Lift, Drag, and Angle of Attack — research packet

## Scope

- **Audience:** U.S. private-pilot airplane learners.
- **Target duration:** 30-35 minutes; approximately 5,000 spoken words at the selected study pace.
- **Core question:** What does a wing do to the air, and how do relative wind, angle of attack, lift, and drag explain the basic choices a pilot makes?
- **In scope:** air as a fluid; relative wind; chord line and angle of attack; lift; induced and parasite drag; the lift/drag tradeoff; pitch attitude versus angle of attack.
- **Out of scope:** maneuver technique, numeric performance calculations, stall recovery, wake-turbulence avoidance procedures, and aircraft-specific speeds or limitations.

## Likely trouble spots

- **Misconception:** “Air over the curved top has to meet air from the bottom at the trailing edge.”
  - **Correction:** The PHAK says lift is more complex than simple differential pressure; both Bernoulli’s principle and Newton’s laws operate whenever an airfoil generates lift.
  - **Source:** `phak-lift-and-airfoil-behavior`.
- **Misconception:** “The nose-high picture tells me the angle of attack.”
  - **Correction:** Angle of attack is the angle between chord line and relative wind, not between chord line and the horizon.
  - **Source:** `phak-aoa-and-relative-wind`.
- **Misconception:** “A familiar published stall speed proves the wing cannot be near its aerodynamic limit.”
  - **Correction:** A published one-G value is useful for its stated conditions; a change in the flight condition can change the airspeed associated with the critical angle of attack.
  - **Source:** `phak-stall-flow`.
- **Key takeaway:** A pilot manages the conditions that preserve useful airflow over the airfoil. Detailed maneuver techniques remain aircraft-specific and belong in later flight instruction.
  - **Source:** `phak-stall-flow`.
- **Misconception:** “Drag is only the airplane pushing through the air.”
  - **Correction:** Parasite drag rises with speed; induced drag is the drag associated with lift and is greatest at high angle of attack and low airspeed.
  - **Source:** `phak-drag-tradeoff`.
